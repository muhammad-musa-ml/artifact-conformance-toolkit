CHECK-16 -- THE RED TRANSCRIPT
===========================================

WHAT CHECK-16 REQUIRES, from its row in the v1 checker contract:

    Fails when a figure the artifact's own results/retractions.json declares
    retracted is still being asserted somewhere in the tree. Every hit is
    CLASSIFIED -- LIVE, FROZEN, FALSE-POSITIVE or UNCLASSIFIED -- because a
    retraction record quoting the figure it withdrew is the record doing its
    job and looks identical to a live claim under a grep. A bare-numeric needle
    adjacent to another digit is a FALSE-POSITIVE, counted and never a finding.
    The declaration's ABSENCE is exit 2; `retracted: []` with a reviewed_on
    date is a legal pass over a real population.

WHY THIS CHECKER EXISTS, AND WHY IT EXISTS NOW
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Its id was RESERVED, not invented: the v2 list carried "CHECK-16:
Retraction-leak grep -- trigger: the first retraction", with the note that a
legacy finding was already one "so this may pull into v1". The trigger fired.
Two figures and one claim were withdrawn from one of this programme's
artifacts, and a one-off sweep counted where they still appeared across two
trees: 17 appearances by (file, line, needle), of which ZERO were live, 14 were
frozen and 3 were false positives.

That sweep closes by saying exactly what it cannot do:

    It is a measurement taken once. Nothing re-runs it. If a retracted figure
    is typed back into a sentence tomorrow, this file will not notice.

This is what re-runs it.

THE SHORTCUT THE WORDING INVITES, AND WHY THIS CHECK REFUSES IT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"a retracted figure that still appears" reads like ONE condition, and a checker
built on it alone is a grep: read the needles, scan the tree, report the hits.
That checker is WRONG IN BOTH DIRECTIONS AT ONCE, and the sweep measured both.

Wrong in the direction that fires: a needle of one withdrawn hit rate matched
`"rps": 6398.8` in a results file -- a throughput reading with nothing to do
with the figure. The sweep's own conclusion is this check's needle rule, and
the reason it matters is not tidiness: a checker that fires on a throughput
number in a results file trains its reader to ignore it, and a checker that is
routinely ignored is worse than none.

Wrong in the direction that stays silent: a retraction RECORD quotes the figure
it withdrew, and so does the withdrawn run's own machine record. Those are the
record doing its job. A checker that called them live would make writing an
honest retraction impossible, and the repair a reader would reach for --
deleting the withdrawn run's files -- destroys the evidence of what was
withdrawn and leaves the retraction pointing at nothing.

So FROZEN and FALSE-POSITIVE are first-class arms with their own tests in both
directions, and there is no exclusion list: the declaration is scanned like
every other file, and its own hits classify FROZEN on the ordinary rule because
a withdrawal date sits beside each of them.

WHAT MAKES THIS A REAL RED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The module is REGISTERED and RUNNABLE in this commit. It walks the artifact,
prunes the directories, opens every text file, counts what it read and what it
did not, and reports a population through the frozen core's own printer. Only
the JUDGEMENT is stubbed: it reads no declaration and classifies no hit.

So every failure below is an assertion about a VERDICT. None of a design rule's five
warning signs appears anywhere in this file -- checked mechanically by the
script that assembled it, with both needles built from fragments so this header
cannot satisfy its own check.

TWO DEFECTS THE IMPLEMENTATION HAD, MEASURED BEFORE IT LANDED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Recorded here because a transcript that only shows the stub failing hides what
the GREEN commit actually had to get right, and both were found by running the
implementation over the committed fixture and reading every hit rather than the
verdict.

  1. A JSON line `"value": 4.321,` classified FALSE-POSITIVE on its TRAILING
     COMMA, because the adjacency rule treated any `.` or `,` as part of a
     longer number. That is the check going blind to a withdrawn figure in a
     machine record -- the single most likely place for one to survive -- while
     printing a clean adjudication. A separator now counts only when a DIGIT
     sits on its far side.

  2. Worse, and the same probe found it: the boundary characters were spelled
     `""` for "off the end of the line", and `"" in "0123456789"` is True in
     Python. EVERY match touching a line boundary was FALSE-POSITIVE. A
     withdrawn figure alone on its own line was invisible.
